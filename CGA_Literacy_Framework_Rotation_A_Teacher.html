Three-Tier Structure

K–2
Picture Book Read-Aloud One shared picture book per unit read aloud by the teacher. All discussion, comprehension, and vocabulary work through that shared text. Oral and drawn responses for K–1.
Grades 3–4
Partner Reading Tiered menu: picture books for developing readers, early chapter books for mid-level, shorter novels for strong readers. Partners read together, discuss, then join whole-class seminar.
Grades 5–6
Solo Independent Reading Full book menu, individual reading journals, full Socratic seminar. Same literary lens and driving question as K–4 with more analytical writing expectations.

Seminar Questions at a Glance

Unit 1 — What does the author want you to feel or believe that they never actually say out loud?
Unit 2 — What makes someone keep going when they don't know if they're right?
Unit 3 — When someone uses their voice against an unfair system, what are they risking — and what makes it worth it?
Unit 4 — When two animals compete for survival, what actually determines who wins — strength, adaptations, or something else?
Unit 5 — What does the story a culture chooses to keep and retell tell us about what that culture believes?

How Rotation A & B Work Together

Rotation B (Hero's Journey, Character Transformation, Point of View, Theme/Belonging) centers on fiction and literary analysis. Rotation A pulls harder on nonfiction, argument, biography, and real-world texts. A student who completes both rotations has read across every major genre, practiced every major CCSS reading and writing standard, and built a complete toolkit for thinking about texts.

K–2 Overview

K–2 students work through the same literary lenses and driving questions as the rest of the class, using shared picture book read-alouds as their primary text. Journal responses are oral, drawn, or dictated for K–1; short written responses for 2nd grade. Socratic seminar is replaced by a structured class share with the same questions in simplified form.

Grades 3–4 Overview

Grades 3–4 use partner reading with a tiered menu per unit. Journal prompts are the same as grades 5–6 but responses are shorter and more scaffolded. Culminating writing is one paragraph with a claim and evidence. Partners read together, discuss, then join the whole-class Socratic seminar with the same driving question as grades 5–6.

Charlotte's Web · Clementine · The One and Only Ivan · Bud Not Buddy (strong readers)

Dec
Gingerbread variants · written story with setting and twist
Unit 3
Who Was? series · Ruth and the Green Book · Number the Stars (strong readers)
Unit 4
Who Would Win? titles · Charlotte's Web · My Side of the Mountain (strong readers)
Unit 5
D'Aulaires' Greek Myths · folklore collections · The Lightning Thief (strong readers)

Grades 5–6 Overview

Grades 5–6 read solo from the full book menu, maintain independent reading journals, and participate in the full Socratic seminar. Culminating writing escalates across the year: paragraph (Unit 1) → short essay (Unit 2) → performance task (Unit 3) → comparative argument essay (Unit 4) → comparative myth essay (Unit 5).
