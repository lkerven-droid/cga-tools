Three-Tier Structure

K–2
Picture Book Read-Aloud One shared picture book read aloud by the teacher. All discussion, comprehension, and vocabulary work through that shared text. Oral and drawn responses for K–1.
Grades 3–4
Partner Reading Tiered menu: picture books for developing readers, early chapter books for mid-level, shorter novels for strong readers. Partners read together, discuss, then join whole-class seminar.
Grades 5–6
Solo Independent Reading Full book menu, individual reading journals, full Socratic seminar. Same literary lens and driving question as K–4 with more analytical writing expectations.

Seminar Questions at a Glance

Unit 1 — Hero's Journey

What does a hero — real or fictional — have to lose before they can truly transform?

Unit 2 — Character & Transformation

Can a character change without wanting to — and is that still transformation?

Unit 3 — Point of View

Whose story isn't being told — in your novel or in the poems we read — and why does that matter?

Unit 4 — Belonging & Identity

Does belonging require you to change who you are, or does it require finding people who accept who you already are?

K–2 Picture Book Anchors

One primary anchor per unit read multiple times. First for enjoyment, second for noticing, third for discussion and response.

Unit 1 — Hero's Journey
Primary: Last Stop on Market Street, Brave Irene, The Empty Pot
Additional: Jabari Jumps, The Adventures of Beekle ⭐, How to Catch a Star
Beekle is especially powerful for framework-building — every hero's journey stage is visible. Use it first to map stages explicitly.
Unit 2 — Character & Transformation
Primary: The Name Jar, Enemy Pie, Each Kindness
Additional: Those Shoes, Fry Bread, When We Were Alone
Each Kindness is powerful because the transformation is incomplete — she misses her chance. Creates rich discussion about what doesn't change and why.
Unit 3 — Point of View
Primary: The True Story of the Three Little Pigs ⭐, Voices in the Park, Wolf!
Additional: Alfie, The Day You Begin, Duck! Rabbit!
True Story of the Three Little Pigs is the universal hook at every grade level. Use this first — always.
Unit 4 — Belonging & Identity
Primary: The Velveteen Rabbit ⭐, Big Al, Each Little Bird That Sings
Additional: The Day You Begin, Hair Love, We Are Water Protectors, The Smallest Girl
The Velveteen Rabbit is the richest anchor — becoming 'real' through love maps directly onto the belonging seminar question. Read it slowly.

Grades 3–4 Partner Reading Menus

Each menu is tiered. Partners choose one book together. They discuss with their partner before joining the whole-class seminar conversation.

Unit 1 — Hero's Journey
Picture book: Brave Irene, The Adventures of Beekle
Early chapter book: My Father's Dragon, Flat Stanley, The Boxcar Children
Shorter novel: Zita the Spacegirl, The Wild Robot, How to Train Your Dragon
Unit 2 — Character & Transformation
Picture book: The Name Jar, Each Kindness
Early chapter book: Charlotte's Web, Because of Winn-Dixie, Shiloh, Sarah Plain and Tall
Shorter novel: Wonder, Holes, Inside Out and Back Again (verse)
Unit 3 — Point of View
Picture book: Voices in the Park, The True Story of the Three Little Pigs
Early chapter book: My Father's Dragon, Nate the Great, Hilo (graphic novel)
Shorter novel: Coraline, The View from Saturday, Counting by 7s
Unit 4 — Belonging & Identity
Picture book: Big Al, The Velveteen Rabbit, The Day You Begin
Early chapter book: Charlotte's Web, Because of Winn-Dixie, Each Little Bird That Sings, The Cay
Shorter novel: Front Desk, The Girl Who Drank the Moon, New Kid (graphic novel)

CCSS Alignment

The anchor standard across all four units is RL.5.9 / RL.6.9 — cross-text comparison. Informational standards are hit authentically through the nonfiction pairings.

Literary Reading (RL)

RL.5.2 / RL.6.2Theme and summary — Units 1, 2
RL.5.3 / RL.6.3Character, setting, plot — Unit 2
RL.5.5 / RL.6.5Story structure — Unit 1
RL.5.6 / RL.6.6Point of view — Unit 3
RL.5.9 / RL.6.9Cross-text comparison — All units ⭐
